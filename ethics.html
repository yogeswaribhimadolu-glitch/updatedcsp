AI ethics and safety

AI is powerful, but it is not perfect and it is not neutral. This page covers the main risks of AI, how they show up in real life, and what you can do to use AI responsibly.

Eight risks of AI

For each risk: what it is, what it looks like, and what you can do.

Responsible AI principles

Companies, universities and governments often describe responsible AI with a similar set of ideas.

Fairness

AI should treat people fairly and not disadvantage groups.

Transparency

People should know when AI is being used and how it reached a result.

Accountability

A human or organisation is always responsible for what an AI system does.

Privacy

Personal data must be collected and used carefully and with consent.

Safety and reliability

AI should be tested and should fail safely.

Human oversight

For important decisions, a person must be able to review and override the AI.

Using AI in your studies

Good uses

  • Asking for an explanation of a topic you find hard
  • Getting practice questions and checking your answers
  • Improving grammar and clarity of your own writing
  • Brainstorming ideas and outlines
  • Getting help to understand an error in your code

Not okay

  • Submitting AI-written work as entirely your own
  • Using AI where your college has banned it
  • Copying answers without understanding them
  • Making up sources or trusting AI citations without checking
  • Uploading classmates' or teachers' private information
Simple rule: If you cannot explain your work without the AI, you have not learned it yet. Follow your college's AI policy and mention where you used AI.
