Prompt engineering

A prompt is the instruction you give to an AI. The clearer your prompt, the better the answer. This page teaches the parts of a good prompt, the main prompting techniques with examples, and lets you build your own prompt.

The parts of a good prompt

A strong prompt usually has five parts. You do not need all five every time, but the more you include, the less the AI has to guess.

Role

Who the AI should act as. It sets the style and depth of the answer.

Example"Act as a friendly computer science tutor."

Task

What exactly you want it to do. Use a clear action word.

Example"Explain how neural networks learn."

Context

Background the AI cannot know: who you are, your level, your goal.

Example"I am a first-year B.Tech student."

Format

How the answer should look: bullets, table, steps, word count.

Example"Give 5 bullet points and one example."

Limits

Rules and boundaries: length, tone, things to avoid.

Example"Under 150 words, simple English, no jargon."

Prompting techniques

These are common ways to get better results. Open each one to see what it is and an example.

Zero-shot prompting
You ask directly with no examples. This works well for simple, common tasks.
Translate this sentence into Hindi: "The exam is on Monday."
Few-shot prompting
You show one or more examples of the input and output you want, so the AI copies the pattern. Very useful for a special format or style.
Turn each sentence into a short headline. Sentence: The college will open a new library next month. Headline: New Library Opens Next Month Sentence: Students can now submit assignments online. Headline:
Chain-of-thought (step by step)
You ask the AI to work through the problem in steps before giving the final answer. This helps with maths, logic and planning, and lets you check the reasoning. Some newer models do this internally, but asking for steps is still useful when you want to verify the answer.
A train travels 180 km in 3 hours. How long will it take to travel 300 km at the same speed? Solve step by step, then give the final answer on its own line.
Role prompting
You give the AI a role so it answers with the right vocabulary and viewpoint.
Act as an interviewer for a junior web developer job. Ask me one question at a time and give feedback after each answer.
Iterative refinement
Your first prompt is a draft. Follow up to improve the result instead of starting over.
Make it shorter. Now add a real-life example. Rewrite it for a Class 8 student.
Prompt chaining
You split a big job into smaller prompts, using each answer as the input for the next.
Prompt 1: List 5 project ideas about AI for students. Prompt 2: Pick idea 3 and create a project outline. Prompt 3: Write a one-page introduction from the outline.

Common mistakes

Being too vague

"Tell me about AI" gives a generic answer. Say what part, for whom and how long.

Asking too many things at once

A prompt with ten different tasks gets a messy answer. Split it into steps.

Not giving context

The AI does not know your level, your course or your goal unless you say so.

Trusting the first answer

Always check facts, numbers and code. Ask the AI to show its sources or reasoning.

Sharing private data

Never put passwords, ID numbers or other people's personal details in a prompt.

Giving up after one try

Follow-up prompts like "make it simpler" usually fix the problem.

Quick tips

Be specific

Say exactly what you want, for whom, and how long.

Show an example

Paste a sample of the style you want.

Ask for a format

Tables, bullet points and steps are easier to scan.

Ask the AI to ask you

"Ask me questions first if you need more information."

Set the audience

"Explain for a Class 10 student" changes the whole answer.

Verify

Treat the answer as a first draft and check it.
